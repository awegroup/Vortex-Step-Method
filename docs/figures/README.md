# Figures: consistent lifting-line coupling

Generated by `make_pr_figures.py` (run from the repository root, a few
minutes; figures 01-07 use rib legs explicitly, the default when they were
made, see 10 and 11 for the leg direction). They document the changes that align the solver with Gaunaa, Li &
Pirrung (TORQUE 2026, "From thin airfoil theory to finite wings"), Li, Gaunaa,
Pirrung & Lønbæk (TORQUE 2026, "Relaxation limits and artificial viscosity")
and Gaunaa, Sørensen & Li (2024, spanwise-flow viscous correction).
`v3_polars_before_fixes.json` holds the V3 polars of the solver before these
changes; the current code cannot reproduce them.

## 01 V3 polars before and after
TUDELFT V3 kite, CAD geometry, CFD+NeuralFoil polars, 50 panels, 2.82 m/s,
against RANS (Poland 2025, Vire 2022) and wind-tunnel data (Poland 2025).
Dashed black: the solver before the changes. Blue: after the changes with the
3/4-chord force directions (the old default). Red: with quarter-chord
directions (`is_aoa_corrected=True`, the paper's LL-Gaunaa, now the default),
dotted without the attached-trailed force. The quarter-chord variant sits on the RANS drag in the
attached range and in sideslip; the 3/4-chord variants overshoot it. The
wind tunnel carries bridle drag and a lower Reynolds number.

## 02 Panel frame, drag direction and viscous-correction angle
Top left: panel axes at the V3 left tip. Blue is the old frame (chord along
the rib, normal from the leading-edge step), red the new span-perpendicular
frame, black the span. Top right: the old frame's chord-to-span skew per
panel and the angle by which the old profile-drag direction (global span axis
crossed with the lift direction) missed the local flow, up to 108 deg at the
tips. Bottom: the spanwise-flow angle beta fed to the Gaunaa 2024 viscous
correction, old (a geometry-only number that never saw the flow) versus new
(from the relative velocity), at 0 and 10 deg sideslip.

## 03 Trefftz-plane check
Inviscid wings, on-blade induced drag divided by the Trefftz-plane induced
drag on the same circulation (`results["drag_induced_trefftz"]`). Quarter-
chord directions with the attached-trailed force and the curved bound-vortex
regularisation (figure 08) reproduce the far-wake drag exactly on straight
wings, within 2% on the elliptic wing and the 30 deg swept wing, and within
2% on the V3 at 5, 10 and 15 deg (before the regularisation the V3 was 7-14%
low and drifting with the panel count). The 3/4-chord directions overshoot
by 20-40%. Without the attached-trailed force the V3 is 5-13% low.

## 04 Attached-trailed vortex force on the V3
Kutta-Joukowski force on the chordwise legs between the bound vortex and the
trailing edge (Gaunaa, Li & Pirrung 2026, Sec. 3), inviscid V3 at 10 deg.
Left: per-boundary force vectors. Right: their vertical and streamwise
components; largest over the curved outboard region, zero at mid-span where
the legs are aligned with the flow. Total 18 N of 760 N lift.

## 05 Adaptive relaxation factor
Left: iterations of the base loop on the V3 with the old fixed 0.01 and with
the default derived from the Li et al. 2026 stability limit (Eq. 11 in
per-panel form), for VSM and LLT. Right: the measured stability limit (from
the fixed-point Jacobian) over the paper's bound on rectangular wings: the
bound is exact for LLT and about half for VSM, whose 3/4-chord control point
sees twice the trailing-vortex induction; the solver halves it for VSM.

## 08 Curved bound vortex at the quarter chord: problem and regularisation
Figures 08 and 09 are generated by `docs/validation/curved_bound_vortex_validation.py --figures`,
which also reproduces every table of `docs/validation/curved_bound_vortex_report.pdf`
(the problem statement, the literature comparison and the verification).
(a) On a spanwise-curved bound line the neighbouring segments induce a
chordwise velocity at the panel's own quarter-chord point that grows as
ln(span / panel width). (b) Arc wing, mid-span: that velocity from the
polyline (grows by 0.05 m/s per doubling of the panel count) and from the
chordwise-spread thin-airfoil sheet (finite, -0.186 m/s); the grey band is
the value at which the on-blade induced drag equals the Trefftz plane.
(c) Why it matters: the on-blade induced drag is rho Gamma (w cos a + u sin a),
and w is only about 1 deg of induced angle, so u = 3% of U moves the drag by
12%. (d) The thin-airfoil distribution used to spread the bound vorticity;
effective cut-off 0.15 c. (e) The construction: classical downwash of the wing
unrolled onto the local tangent line, plus the chord-regularised difference
between the actual and the unrolled systems. (f) On-blade over Trefftz drag
against panel count on the arc and the inviscid V3, before (dashed) and
after (solid).

## 09 V3 polars with the curved bound-vortex regularisation
TUDELFT V3 kite, CAD geometry, 2D RANS Re 5e5 polars, 50 panels, 2.82 m/s.
Grey dashed: the solver before this work (3/4-chord directions). Blue:
quarter-chord directions with the other panels' bound segments evaluated on
the line (log-singular). Red: with the regularisation. Lift is unchanged;
drag rises by a fraction of the induced drag, most at high lift and in
sideslip, onto the RANS values up to 10 deg. The wind-tunnel gap that remains
is shared by RANS.

## 06 Wake direction under a yaw rate
Top view of the wake origins with the old single wake direction (mean
freestream before the rotational term) and the new per-ring direction (each
panel's own apparent velocity). Identical in uniform inflow; under body rates
the wake follows the local inflow. A yaw rate mostly changes the local speed,
so the direction differences are small even at the exaggerated 1 rad/s.

## 10 Direction of the attached-trailed legs: geometry
Generated by `docs/validation/leg_direction_study.py --figures`. Top views
(flow downwards) and one side view of the three leg constructions of
`BodyAerodynamics(leg_direction=...)`: along the input ribs (blue, the
default until October 2026), along the flow projected onto the surface
(green, now the default), or
perpendicular to the quarter-chord line as in the TORQUE 2026 paper (red),
with the bound vortex (black), the aerodynamic centres (dots), the control
points (crosses in the colour of their legs) and, in grey, the lattice of the
vortex-lattice referee (AeroSandbox, coarse). (a) A straight wing whose ribs
are drawn 22 deg skewed: rib legs follow the drawing, the other two end on
the trailing-edge line and give the straight-wing result. (b) The root of a
kinked 30 deg swept wing: streamwise ribs against the perpendicular fan,
whose legs would cross at the kink without the tangent averaging. (c) The
swept-anhedral elliptic wing. (d) The arc wing from the side: streamwise
legs climb along the nearly vertical tip at the angle of attack. (e, f) The
V3: the ribs are nearly streamwise over the middle and skewed at the tips,
where the perpendicular legs become long and end far inboard.

## 11 Direction of the attached-trailed legs: referee
Generated by the same script from `vlm_referee.json`
(`docs/validation/vlm_referee.py`, AeroSandbox VLM with flat-plate sections,
chordwise 16, spanwise 80 to 160) and `leg_direction_study.json`. Top: strip
circulation of the VLM and of the VSM with the three leg options on the
kinked swept wing, the elliptic wing and the inviscid V3. Bottom left and
middle: lift and on-blade induced drag of the VSM relative to the VLM (its
Trefftz-plane drag, since its near-field drag with trailing legs along the
body axis is low on the non-planar wings). Rib and streamwise legs are
within 1% in lift except the streamwise arc (2% low); perpendicular legs
lose 9% on the kinked wing and 8% on the V3. Bottom right: V3 viscous drag
against RANS for the three options.
